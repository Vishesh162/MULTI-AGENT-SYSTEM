**Research Report: The State of Artificial Intelligence 2024–2025**  

---

### Introduction  

The period spanning 2024 to 2025 marks a definitive inflection point for artificial intelligence. AI has transitioned from a niche research domain to the dominant driver of global venture capital, enterprise strategy, and regulatory agendas. Industry now produces nearly 90% of notable AI models, up from 60% in 2023 [1], while global private investment surged past $100 billion in 2024—an 80% year‑over‑year increase [3]. Simultaneously, the physical infrastructure underpinning this growth—data centres, power grids, and semiconductor supply chains—is straining under unprecedented demand: data‑centre electricity use jumped 17% in 2025 alone, with AI‑focused facilities growing even faster [6]. Governments worldwide have responded with a ninefold increase in legislative mentions of AI since 2016 and 59 new U.S. federal regulations in 2024 [1].  

This report synthesises empirical data from ten authoritative sources published between 2024 and 2025, covering investment flows, model development trends, enterprise adoption, energy consumption, safety assessments, and regulatory developments. The scope is limited to *general‑purpose* and *generative* AI systems; narrow, task‑specific applications (e.g., traditional computer‑vision pipelines in manufacturing) are excluded unless they intersect with the above themes. “Notable models” refers to the benchmark‑tracked systems catalogued in the Stanford AI Index (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude 3, Llama 3, Gemini 1.5). All monetary figures are in nominal U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted.

### Key Findings  

#### 1. Industry Consolidation of Model Development at Unprecedented Scale  
Nearly 90% of notable AI models released in 2024 originated from industry labs, a sharp rise from 60% in 2023 [1]. This concentration is mirrored in the compute trajectory: training compute for frontier models now doubles approximately every **5 months**, dataset size every **8 months**, and associated power consumption **annually** [1]. The five largest technology companies collectively deployed **> $400 billion** in capital expenditure in 2025, with a further **75% increase projected for 2026** [6]. Such exponential resource demands create a structural barrier to entry, effectively limiting frontier model development to a handful of well‑capitalised firms and raising concerns about market concentration, research diversity, and geopolitical dependency.

#### 2. Venture Capital Reorientation Toward Infrastructure and Late‑Stage Generative AI  
Global venture capital (VC) investment in AI exceeded **$100 billion in 2024** (up from $55.6 billion in 2023), capturing **33% of all global venture funding** and making AI the leading sector [3]. Within AI, the *IT infrastructure/hosting* category attracted **$47.4 billion in 2024** and **$109.3 billion in 2025**, accounting for over two‑thirds of all AI VC since 2023 [2]. Generative AI alone drew **~$45 billion in 2024**, nearly doubling from $24 billion in 2023 [3]. Late‑stage deal sizes for generative AI startups ballooned from a median of **$48 million (2023) to $327 million (2024)**—a 6.8× increase [3]. Notably, AI companies represented **~48% of total equity funding in 2025 while comprising only 23% of deals**, signalling extreme winner‑take‑all dynamics and potential bubble risk in late‑stage valuations [3].

#### 3. Enterprise Adoption Shifts from Experimentation to Scaled Deployment  
McKinsey’s 2025 global survey of 1,500+ organisations shows **78% reporting regular AI use in at least one function in 2024, rising to 88% in 2025** [7]. The share of organisations *scaling* AI across the enterprise jumped to **38% in 2025**, while **51% now use AI in three or more business functions** [7]. Large enterprises (revenues > $10 billion) lead adoption, with healthcare/biotech emerging as a high‑growth vertical for AI integration [3]. This trajectory indicates a transition from pilot‑phase curiosity to value‑capture mode, but also surfaces integration challenges: data governance, talent scarcity, and ROI measurement remain top barriers for the 62% not yet scaling.

#### 4. Energy Demand Growth Outpaces Infrastructure Lead Times, Creating Localised Bottlenecks  
Data‑centre electricity demand surged **17% in 2025**, far outpacing global electricity demand growth of **3%** [6]. AI‑focused data centres are the primary driver; their power use is projected to **triple by 2030**, while total data‑centre consumption is expected to **double** [6]. In absolute terms, AI consumed **~0.5% of world electricity in 2025** (non‑AI data centres used >2× as much) [9]. However, national averages mask severe *local* concentration: **5% of U.S. electricity** already powers data centres, with **~2% attributable to AI‑focused facilities** [9]. Supply‑chain bottlenecks—gas turbines, transformers, advanced chips, and grid interconnection queues—now constrain expansion more than capital availability [6].
